# AHA/ACC 2025 Guideline for the Prevention, Detection, Evaluation, and Management of High Blood Pressure

## Introduction
The 2025 American Heart Association (AHA) and American College of Cardiology (ACC) guidelines provide a comprehensive framework for managing high blood pressure (hypertension) in adults to reduce cardiovascular risk.

## Blood Pressure Classification
Blood pressure (BP) should be categorized into four levels based on the average of two or more careful readings obtained on two or more occasions:
1. **Normal:** Systolic < 120 mmHg AND Diastolic < 80 mmHg.
2. **Elevated:** Systolic 120–129 mmHg AND Diastolic < 80 mmHg.
3. **Stage 1 Hypertension:** Systolic 130–139 mmHg OR Diastolic 80–89 mmHg.
4. **Stage 2 Hypertension:** Systolic ≥ 140 mmHg OR Diastolic ≥ 90 mmHg.

## Measurement and Monitoring
Accurate BP measurement is critical.
- **In-office:** Use validated automated oscillometric devices. Patients should rest for 5 minutes before measurement.
- **Out-of-office:** Out-of-office measurements, such as ambulatory blood pressure monitoring (ABPM) and home blood pressure monitoring (HBPM), are highly recommended to confirm the diagnosis of hypertension and titrate BP-lowering medication.
- **White Coat Hypertension:** Consider in patients with elevated office BP but normal out-of-office BP.
- **Masked Hypertension:** Consider in patients with normal office BP but elevated out-of-office BP.

## Nonpharmacological Interventions
Lifestyle modifications are the cornerstone of hypertension management:
- **Weight loss:** Target BMI of 18.5–24.9 kg/m². Expect ~1 mmHg reduction per 1 kg lost.
- **DASH Diet:** Dietary Approaches to Stop Hypertension (rich in fruits, vegetables, whole grains, low in sodium).
- **Sodium reduction:** Target < 1500 mg/day, but at least a 1000 mg/day reduction is beneficial.
- **Potassium supplementation:** Aim for 3500–5000 mg/day, preferably by diet (unless contraindicated by CKD or drugs).
- **Physical activity:** Aerobic (90–150 min/week), dynamic resistance, and isometric resistance exercises.
- **Alcohol intake:** Limit to ≤ 2 drinks daily for men and ≤ 1 drink daily for women.

## Pharmacological Treatment Thresholds
Initiation of BP-lowering medication depends on BP stage and cardiovascular risk (e.g., 10-year ASCVD risk).
- **Elevated BP:** Nonpharmacological therapy. Reassess in 3–6 months.
- **Stage 1 Hypertension (ASCVD risk < 10%):** Nonpharmacological therapy. Reassess in 3–6 months.
- **Stage 1 Hypertension (ASCVD risk ≥ 10% or known CVD/DM/CKD):** Nonpharmacological therapy + BP-lowering medication (1 agent). Reassess in 1 month.
- **Stage 2 Hypertension:** Nonpharmacological therapy + BP-lowering medication (often requires 2 agents from different classes). Reassess in 1 month.

## Target Blood Pressure Goals
- For adults with confirmed hypertension and known CVD or 10-year ASCVD event risk of 10% or higher, a BP target of < 130/80 mmHg is recommended.
- For adults with confirmed hypertension without additional markers of increased CVD risk, a BP target of < 130/80 mmHg may be reasonable.

## First-Line Antihypertensive Agents
Four primary classes of medications are recommended for initial monotherapy:
1. **Thiazide or thiazide-like diuretics** (e.g., Chlorthalidone, Hydrochlorothiazide). Chlorthalidone is preferred due to prolonged half-life and proven trial reduction of CVD.
2. **Angiotensin-converting enzyme (ACE) inhibitors** (e.g., Lisinopril, Enalapril).
3. **Angiotensin receptor blockers (ARBs)** (e.g., Losartan, Valsartan).
4. **Calcium channel blockers (CCBs)** (e.g., Amlodipine - dihydropyridine).

*Note: ACE inhibitors and ARBs should NOT be used simultaneously.*

## Special Populations
- **Black adults with hypertension:** Initial therapy should include a thiazide-type diuretic or CCB.
- **Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD):** Initial therapy should include an ACE inhibitor or ARB to slow kidney disease progression.
- **Diabetes Mellitus:** Initiate treatment at BP ≥ 130/80 mmHg. All first-line classes are useful and effective.
- **Heart Failure (HFrEF):** ACE inhibitors or ARBs, beta-blockers, and mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists (MRAs) are standard. Avoid non-dihydropyridine CCBs (verapamil, diltiazem).
- **Pregnancy:** Methyldopa, nifedipine, and labetalol are preferred. ACE inhibitors, ARBs, and direct renin inhibitors are contraindicated.

## Resistant Hypertension
Defined as office BP ≥ 130/80 mmHg in a patient prescribed ≥ 3 antihypertensive medications at optimal doses, including a diuretic, or BP controlled on ≥ 4 medications.
- Exclude pseudoresistance (e.g., poor technique, white coat effect, nonadherence).
- Identify and reverse contributing factors (e.g., NSAIDs, stimulants, oral contraceptives, high sodium).
- Screen for secondary causes (e.g., primary aldosteronism, CKD, obstructive sleep apnea, renal artery stenosis).
- Pharmacological strategy: Maximize diuretic therapy, add a mineralocorticoid receptor antagonist (spironolactone), or add agents with different mechanisms of action.

## Hypertensive Crises
- **Hypertensive Emergencies:** Severe BP elevation (usually >180/120 mmHg) with new or worsening target organ damage (e.g., encephalopathy, MI, pulmonary edema). Requires immediate admission to an ICU and intravenous medications for controlled BP reduction.
- **Hypertensive Urgencies:** Severe BP elevation without target organ damage. Manage by adjusting oral maintenance therapy; usually does not require hospital admission.

## Disclaimer
These guidelines are summarized for clinical decision support. Clinicians should use their judgment in individualizing treatment based on patient-specific factors.
