# ADA 2026 Standards of Medical Care in Diabetes: Screening and Diagnosis

## Introduction
This document outlines the 2026 guidelines from the American Diabetes Association (ADA) regarding the screening, diagnosis, and classification of diabetes mellitus and prediabetes.

## Criteria for Diagnosing Diabetes
Diabetes is diagnosed based on any of the following criteria:
1. **Fasting Plasma Glucose (FPG):** ≥ 126 mg/dL (7.0 mmol/L). Fasting is defined as no caloric intake for at least 8 hours.
2. **2-hour Plasma Glucose (PG):** ≥ 200 mg/dL (11.1 mmol/L) during a 75-g oral glucose tolerance test (OGTT).
3. **A1C:** ≥ 6.5% (48 mmol/mol). The test should be performed in a laboratory using a method that is NGSP certified.
4. **Random Plasma Glucose:** ≥ 200 mg/dL (11.1 mmol/L) in a patient with classic symptoms of hyperglycemia or hyperglycemic crisis.

*Note: In the absence of unequivocal hyperglycemia, criteria 1–3 require two abnormal test results from the same sample or in two separate test samples.*

## Criteria for Diagnosing Prediabetes
Prediabetes indicates an increased risk for diabetes and cardiovascular disease. Criteria include:
- **Fasting Plasma Glucose (FPG):** 100 mg/dL (5.6 mmol/L) to 125 mg/dL (6.9 mmol/L) (Impaired Fasting Glucose, IFG).
- **2-hour PG during 75-g OGTT:** 140 mg/dL (7.8 mmol/L) to 199 mg/dL (11.0 mmol/L) (Impaired Glucose Tolerance, IGT).
- **A1C:** 5.7–6.4% (39–47 mmol/mol).

## Screening for Type 2 Diabetes in Asymptomatic Adults
Screening is recommended to detect prediabetes and type 2 diabetes in asymptomatic people.
1. Testing should be considered in adults of any age with overweight or obesity (BMI ≥ 25 kg/m², or ≥ 23 kg/m² in Asian Americans) who have one or more risk factors.
2. For all other patients, testing should begin at age 35 years.
3. If results are normal, testing should be repeated at a minimum of 3-year intervals, with consideration of more frequent testing depending on initial results and risk status.
4. People with prediabetes (A1C ≥ 5.7%, IGT, or IFG) should be tested yearly.
5. Women who were diagnosed with gestational diabetes mellitus should have lifelong testing at least every 3 years.

## Risk Factors for Type 2 Diabetes
- First-degree relative with diabetes.
- High-risk race/ethnicity (e.g., African American, Latino, Native American, Asian American, Pacific Islander).
- History of CVD.
- Hypertension (≥ 130/80 mmHg or on therapy for hypertension).
- HDL cholesterol level < 35 mg/dL and/or a triglyceride level > 250 mg/dL.
- Polycystic ovary syndrome.
- Physical inactivity.
- Conditions associated with insulin resistance (e.g., severe obesity, acanthosis nigricans).

## Screening for Type 1 Diabetes
Screening for type 1 diabetes risk with a panel of islet autoantibodies is recommended in the setting of a research trial or can be offered as an option for first-degree family members of a proband with type 1 diabetes.

## Gestational Diabetes Mellitus (GDM)
- Test for undiagnosed diabetes at the first prenatal visit in those with risk factors.
- Test for GDM at 24–28 weeks of gestation in pregnant women not previously found to have diabetes.
- Screen women with a history of GDM for prediabetes or diabetes at 4–12 weeks postpartum, using the 75-g OGTT.

## Cystic Fibrosis-Related Diabetes (CFRD)
Annual screening for CFRD with an OGTT should begin by age 10 years in all patients with cystic fibrosis not previously diagnosed with CFRD. A1C is not recommended as a screening test for CFRD.

## Post-Transplantation Diabetes Mellitus (PTDM)
Patients should be screened after organ transplantation for PTDM. An OGTT is the preferred test to make a diagnosis of PTDM. A1C can be used, but only after clinical stability has been achieved.

## Monogenic Diabetes Syndromes
All children diagnosed with diabetes in the first 6 months of life should have immediate genetic testing for neonatal diabetes. Consider testing for monogenic diabetes (MODY) in children and adults diagnosed in early adulthood with strong family history but without typical features of type 1 or type 2 diabetes.

## Point-of-Care Testing
Point-of-care A1C assays may be used for screening and diagnosis of diabetes in clinics that hold a CLIA waiver and use an FDA-approved device for diagnosis.

## Disclaimer
These guidelines are summarized for clinical decision support and are not a substitute for clinical judgement. Always verify with full ADA documentation.
