# AHA/ACC/HFSA 2022 Guideline for the Management of Heart Failure

## Introduction
This document summarizes the 2022 guidelines from the American Heart Association (AHA), American College of Cardiology (ACC), and Heart Failure Society of America (HFSA) for the diagnosis and management of heart failure (HF).

## Heart Failure Classification
Heart failure is classified by Left Ventricular Ejection Fraction (LVEF):
1. **HFrEF (Heart Failure with Reduced Ejection Fraction):** LVEF ≤ 40%.
2. **HFmrEF (Heart Failure with Mildly Reduced Ejection Fraction):** LVEF 41% to 49%.
3. **HFpEF (Heart Failure with Preserved Ejection Fraction):** LVEF ≥ 50%.
4. **HFimpEF (Heart Failure with Improved Ejection Fraction):** Previous LVEF ≤ 40% and a follow-up measurement of LVEF > 40%.

## Stages of Heart Failure
- **Stage A (At Risk for HF):** Patients at risk for HF but without current or prior symptoms or signs of HF and without structural, biomarker, or genetic markers of heart disease.
- **Stage B (Pre-HF):** Patients without current or prior symptoms or signs of HF but evidence of structural heart disease, increased filling pressures, or elevated risk biomarkers.
- **Stage C (Symptomatic HF):** Patients with current or prior symptoms and/or signs of HF caused by a structural and/or functional cardiac abnormality.
- **Stage D (Advanced HF):** Severe symptoms and/or signs of HF at rest, recurrent hospitalizations despite guideline-directed medical therapy (GDMT).

## Guideline-Directed Medical Therapy (GDMT) for HFrEF
The core pharmacologic foundation for patients with HFrEF (Stage C) consists of 4 classes of medications:
1. **ARNI (Angiotensin Receptor-Neprilysin Inhibitor):** Sacubitril/valsartan is recommended to reduce morbidity and mortality. If ARNI is not feasible, an ACE inhibitor or ARB is recommended.
2. **Beta-Blockers:** Specifically bisoprolol, carvedilol, or sustained-release metoprolol succinate to reduce mortality and hospitalizations.
3. **MRA (Mineralocorticoid Receptor Antagonist):** Spironolactone or eplerenone are recommended in patients with NYHA class II-IV symptoms to reduce morbidity and mortality. (Monitor potassium and renal function closely).
4. **SGLT2 Inhibitors:** Dapagliflozin or empagliflozin are recommended for patients with symptomatic chronic HFrEF, regardless of the presence of diabetes, to reduce hospitalization for HF and cardiovascular mortality.

## Management of HFmrEF and HFpEF
- **Diuretics:** Recommended for relief of symptoms due to volume overload.
- **SGLT2 Inhibitors:** Recommended in HFmrEF and HFpEF to decrease HF hospitalizations and cardiovascular mortality.
- **MRAs, ARBs, and ARNIs:** Can be considered in HFmrEF and selected patients with HFpEF (particularly those with LVEF on the lower end of the spectrum) to decrease hospitalizations.

## Diuretic Therapy
- Loop diuretics (e.g., furosemide, torsemide, bumetanide) are recommended in patients with HF and fluid retention to relieve symptoms, improve exercise capacity, and reduce hospitalizations.
- Dosage should be carefully titrated to achieve dry weight while avoiding over-diuresis, which can worsen renal function.

## Devices and Interventions
- **ICD (Implantable Cardioverter-Defibrillator):** Recommended for primary prevention of sudden cardiac death in selected patients with nonischemic dilated cardiomyopathy or ischemic heart disease at least 40 days post-MI with LVEF ≤ 35% and NYHA class II or III symptoms on chronic GDMT.
- **CRT (Cardiac Resynchronization Therapy):** Indicated for patients with LVEF ≤ 35%, sinus rhythm, LBBB with a QRS duration of ≥ 150 ms, and NYHA class II, III, or ambulatory IV symptoms on GDMT.

## Hospitalization and Discharge Transitions
- Patients hospitalized for HF should be carefully evaluated for precipitating factors and optimization of GDMT prior to discharge.
- Early follow-up, ideally within 7 to 14 days of discharge, is critical to assess volume status, adjust GDMT, and prevent 30-day readmissions.
- Multidisciplinary HF disease management programs are recommended for patients at high risk for hospital readmission.

## Disclaimer
These guidelines are summarized for clinical decision support. Always consult full AHA/ACC documentation and individualize treatment plans based on patient comorbidities.
