# ADA 2026 Standards of Medical Care: Pharmacologic Approaches to Glycemic Treatment

## Introduction
This document summarizes the American Diabetes Association (ADA) 2026 guidelines for the pharmacologic management of adults with type 2 diabetes.

## Glycemic Targets
- **A1C Goal:** A general target of < 7.0% (53 mmol/mol) for many nonpregnant adults is appropriate.
- **More Stringent Goals (e.g., < 6.5%):** For patients with a short duration of diabetes, type 2 diabetes treated with lifestyle or metformin only, long life expectancy, or no significant CVD, provided it can be achieved without significant hypoglycemia.
- **Less Stringent Goals (e.g., < 8.0%):** For patients with a history of severe hypoglycemia, limited life expectancy, advanced microvascular or macrovascular complications, extensive comorbid conditions, or long-standing diabetes in whom the general target is difficult to achieve.

## First-Line Therapy
- Metformin remains the preferred initial pharmacological agent for the treatment of type 2 diabetes.
- Once initiated, metformin should be continued as long as it is tolerated and not contraindicated.
- Other medications (glucagon-like peptide 1 (GLP-1) receptor agonists, sodium-glucose cotransporter 2 (SGLT2) inhibitors), with or without metformin, are considered appropriate initial therapy for individuals with type 2 diabetes with or at high risk for atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease (ASCVD), heart failure (HF), or chronic kidney disease (CKD).

## Cardiovascular Disease (ASCVD)
For patients with established ASCVD or indicators of high ASCVD risk:
- **GLP-1 Receptor Agonists:** Preferred agents include dulaglutide, liraglutide, and semaglutide (subcutaneous).
- **SGLT2 Inhibitors:** Preferred agents include empagliflozin, canagliflozin, and dapagliflozin.
- These agents should be considered independently of baseline A1C or individualized A1C target.

## Heart Failure (HF)
For patients with type 2 diabetes and established heart failure (particularly HFrEF):
- **SGLT2 Inhibitors:** Empagliflozin, canagliflozin, and dapagliflozin have proven benefit in reducing heart failure hospitalizations and should be added to the regimen.

## Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD)
For patients with type 2 diabetes and CKD (e.g., eGFR < 60 mL/min/1.73 m² or urinary albumin-to-creatinine ratio (UACR) ≥ 300 mg/g):
- **SGLT2 Inhibitors:** Preferred to reduce CKD progression and cardiovascular events.
- If an SGLT2 inhibitor is contraindicated or not tolerated, a **GLP-1 Receptor Agonist** with proven cardiovascular benefit is recommended.

## Weight Management
When choosing glucose-lowering medications, consider their effect on weight:
- **Weight Loss Promoted:** GLP-1 receptor agonists (e.g., semaglutide, tirzepatide) and SGLT2 inhibitors.
- **Weight Neutral:** Metformin, DPP-4 inhibitors.
- **Weight Gain Associated:** Insulin, sulfonylureas, thiazolidinediones (TZDs).

## Insulin Therapy
- The early introduction of insulin should be considered if there is evidence of ongoing catabolism (weight loss), if symptoms of hyperglycemia are present, or when A1C levels (> 10% [86 mmol/mol]) or blood glucose levels (≥ 300 mg/dL [16.7 mmol/L]) are very high.
- **Basal Insulin:** Typically the starting insulin regimen. Includes long-acting analogs (glargine, detemir, degludec) or intermediate-acting human NPH.
- **Prandial Insulin:** If A1C remains above target despite adequately titrated basal insulin, consider adding prandial (mealtime) insulin.
- When insulin is initiated, metformin should generally be continued for glycemic and metabolic benefits.
- Sulfonylureas should typically be discontinued or dose-reduced when prandial insulin is initiated to avoid severe hypoglycemia.

## Hypoglycemia Management
- Individuals at risk for hypoglycemia should be asked about symptomatic and asymptomatic hypoglycemia at every encounter.
- Glucose (15–20 g) is the preferred treatment for the conscious individual with blood glucose < 70 mg/dL (3.9 mmol/L).
- Glucagon should be prescribed for all individuals at increased risk of level 2 or 3 hypoglycemia.

## Disclaimer
These guidelines are summarized for clinical decision support. Treatment must be individualized. Always verify with full ADA documentation.
